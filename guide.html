Getting started

Steppe Crawl is a turn-based roguelike set in Irad. You ride out from the Qara-Chik road into ten valleys, each a day farther from home. Nothing moves until you do: every step, swing or spell takes time, and then the creatures around you act.

Your goal

Under Manas Peak, ten days from home, a sealed cleft leads to the Hidden Valley of Janyl and the ruin of the First Church. There the bull-headed Herald of Morkhan keeps the Ene Malka, the Mother Leaven of St. Janyl. The cleft opens only for someone carrying three clan tamgas (}), which lie at the far ends of four wild regions off the road. Win by carrying the leaven all the way back up the Qara-Chik road.

Your first few minutes

  1. Pick a people and a calling. For a first game, try an Iradi Baatyr (tough, simple) or a Tash-Kishi Kashysh (a priest who can heal).
  2. Press o to explore. You stop automatically when something comes into view.
  3. Press Tab to fight the nearest foe, or walk into it.
  4. When hurt, step away from danger and press 5 to rest.
  5. When the valley is explored, press > to travel to the way on, and > again to take it.

Death is permanent

As in Crawl, when you die the game is over and your save is deleted. You get a character file summarising your journey. Finished games also appear on the scoreboard on the start page (you can turn this off there). The game saves automatically in your browser as you play, so you can close the tab and continue later from the title screen.

Two ways to play

The main game uses graphical tiles. A classic ASCII version (ascii.html) plays exactly the same and shares your saved game.

Aiming

Spells, wands, bows and abilities that need a target open a cursor on the nearest foe. Tab moves to the next foe, arrows move the cursor, Enter or . fires, Esc cancels. The highlighted path shows where a bolt will go.

Mouse and touch

Click or tap a place on the map to travel there; tap next to you to step or attack; tap yourself to pick up. While aiming, tap a target once to move the cursor and again to fire. On a phone, use the arrow pad and the buttons under the map; More has every command.

Skills and experience

Killing creatures gives experience. Experience raises your experience level (more health and magic) and is shared out between the skills you are training.

  • Press m to see your skills. Press a skill's letter to cycle it between off, train and focus (a double share).
  • With automatic training on (the default), any skill you use switches itself on. Turn off skills you don't want so experience isn't wasted.
  • Every third experience level you choose +1 Strength, Intelligence or Dexterity.

Combat

  • AC (armour class) reduces the damage of each hit. EV (evasion) makes you harder to hit. SH (shield) lets you block blows entirely.
  • Heavy armour gives more AC but lowers EV, slows spells, and makes you noisier. Training Armour reduces the penalty.
  • Each weapon has a delay; skill lowers it. A big axe hits hard but slowly until you have trained Axes.
  • Bows and slings need ammunition (f fires, Q chooses). Javelins, darts and stones can be thrown without a launcher.
  • Many creatures start asleep (a blue line under them). Stealth helps you slip past or strike first.
  • The monster list colours each creature by threat: grey easy, white fair, yellow dangerous, red deadly. Use x and Enter to read about one.
  • Fighting in a corridor or doorway means only one foe can reach you at a time.
  • Resistances (shown on %) cut damage from fire, cold, poison, lightning and Morkhan's dark magic.

Magic

Spells come from books (+). Picking one up adds its spells to your library; M memorises them. Each spell uses as many spell levels as its level, and you gain more spell levels with experience and Spellcasting.

  • Casting costs magic (MP) equal to the spell's level. Magic comes back slowly over time.
  • Every spell has a failure chance, shown in the cast menu. It falls with school skill, Spellcasting and Intelligence, and rises with heavy armour and shields.
  • Power sets how strong a spell is, up to a cap. Magic staves add half again to their school.
  • Hexes (sleep, slow, fear, charm) can be resisted; strong-willed and mindless creatures often shrug them off.

Patrons

Pray (p) at an altar to give yourself to a patron. Altars are scattered through the valleys, and the White Yurts have one for every patron. Patrons grow fonder of you (piety, shown as stars) when you do what they like, and grant powers you use with a. If you leave a patron, they stay angry for a while.

Tips

  • Rest to full health before taking the way on. The next valley's creatures will not wait for you.
  • Retreating is not cowardice. Go back the way you came, fight in a narrow spot, or leave a fight you are losing.
  • Try unknown flasks and scrolls when you are healthy and safe, not in an emergency.
  • Kymyz cures poison; ak süt heals deep wounds. Keep a few.
  • Pick a patron early. The White Yurts, a few days from home, have every altar.
  • Focus your experience on a few skills rather than many.
  • Uniques (marked in pink) are much tougher than their kind. It is fine to come back for them later.
  • Black bulls, horned cultists and the dead serve Morkhan; St. Janyl's holy light and juniper smoke hurt them most.
  • The regions get much harder than the valleys around them. If a region is too much, come back when you are stronger.

Steppe Crawl is a roguelike set in Irad, in the tradition of Dungeon Crawl Stone Soup and of Kuble Soft's 1979 caravan game Köch: The Road to Iyık Janyl. Tiles: the public-domain Dungeon Crawl 32x32 tiles. Prefer ASCII? Play the ASCII version.
